# Brand, content and SEO audit

Written 2026-11-13 from the brief "Global brand, content & SEO master audit". It covers every customer-facing page, the emails, and how search engines see the site.

Two limits to know before reading:

- **Section 4 of the brief ("International SEO strategy") arrived as a heading with no text.** The SEO section below is written from the site itself. If the brief had requirements there, send them and this section will be revised.
- **No search data was available.** There are no keyword volumes, rankings or competitor figures in this document, because the shop is not live and no keyword tool or Search Console is connected. Where a search term is named, it is a theme to test, not a measured opportunity.

What was changed on the site as part of this audit is listed in section 7. Everything else is a recommendation.

---

## 1. Summary

**What is already strong.** The site has a clear, unusual promise and keeps it: every bottle has a lot number, and nothing is claimed that cannot be shown. The writing is plain and specific. There are no invented awards, health claims or histories. That honesty is the brand's best asset and most competitors cannot copy it.

**What is weak.**

1. **The two people are announced but not present.** The site says "a husband and wife" several times, but there are no names, no photograph, no reason they started, no voice of their own. A reader is told there is a family and never meets it. This is the largest gap between the brief and the site, and only the owners can close it (section 6).
2. **The tone was honest but cool.** It read like a careful auditor, not a couple inviting you to their table. Words like "the house" and "the administrator" kept the reader at a distance.
3. **There is almost nothing to sell yet.** One product, described as a placeholder, drawn rather than photographed, with no producer, no tasting notes and no laboratory report. No amount of copy fixes this; it needs real catalogue data.
4. **Unfinished parts are visible to customers.** Placeholder reviews, draft bands, "to be confirmed" payment methods, bracketed legal text. All correct before launch, all must go before it.
5. **Search engines can only read the shop in English.** The home page, the editorial pages and the FAQ exist in English only, so the German, Dutch, French, Italian and Spanish addresses have no page of their own to rank.
6. **There is no educational content.** People search for what extra virgin means, what polyphenols are, how to store oil. The site answered none of it. (The FAQ now does, briefly.)

## 2. Positioning

**One sentence.** Azzopardi is a husband and wife who choose high-polyphenol extra virgin olive oils from around the Mediterranean, following each country's harvest season, produce a house blend of their own, and show you the laboratory report for the bottle in your hand.

**Why a customer should believe it.**

| Promise | Proof on the site |
| --- | --- |
| Chosen by two people, not a buyer's desk | The story page — once it has their names and faces |
| High in polyphenols | The verified laboratory figure on each oil's page, with the laboratory and date |
| You know where it is from | Country, region and producer read from the catalogue; a lot number on every label |
| Nothing is exaggerated | A dash wherever something is not recorded; no claims without a document |

**Who it is for.** People who cook, read labels, and have been disappointed by supermarket oil. They do not need to be told olive oil is healthy or luxurious. They want to know what is in the bottle and who stands behind it.

**What it is not.** Not only a selector: the owners also produce olive oil, to be sold as a house blend (told on 2026-11-13, after this audit was first written). Until they have said where and how, the site says so in one sentence and does not describe a grove, a harvest or a mill of their own. Not a wellness brand (no health promises). Not a luxury brand in the gold-and-adjectives sense: the luxury is the care and the evidence.

## 3. Brand voice: family, plain, generous

### The idea

Two people who care about this are showing you what they found. They speak the way they would across a table: directly, warmly, without selling.

### Rules

1. **Say "we", and mean the two of you.** Not "the house", "the company", "our team", "the administrator".
2. **One specific fact beats three adjectives.** "Tested at 512 mg/kg on 3 March" (when it is true) instead of "exceptionally rich".
3. **Say what you do not know.** "Not recorded yet" is a sentence the brand is proud of.
4. **Invite, do not push.** "See the oils" rather than "Buy now". No countdowns, no "limited", no exclamation marks.
5. **Short sentences. Everyday words.** If a sentence would sound odd said aloud at dinner, rewrite it.
6. **Warmth comes from attention, not from sentiment.** Thank people by name. Tell them what happens next. Do not write "we are passionate about".
7. **High-polyphenol is stated, measured, and left there.** Never followed by what it does for the body.

### Words

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| we, the two of us, our table | the house, the company, our team |
| chosen, tasted, measured, recorded | curated, crafted, artisanal, hand-selected |
| high-polyphenol, high in polyphenols | superfood, antioxidant-rich, healthy, medicinal |
| producer (by name) | our farmers, our growers, our groves |
| a dash means we have not recorded it | N/A, coming soon, TBC |
| good, fresh, peppery, bitter, green | exquisite, finest, premium, luxurious, world-class |

### The voice in each place

| Place | How it sounds | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home page | A welcome, then the proof | "We are a husband and wife who choose high-polyphenol extra virgin olive oils from around the Mediterranean, one lot at a time." |
| Product page | Facts first, then how it tastes and what to eat it with | "From [producer], [region]. Picked in [year]. Peppery, with a bitter finish; we like it on white beans." (Every bracket from the record.) |
| Newsletter | A letter, written by one of you, signed with a first name | "The new harvest from [producer] arrived on Tuesday. We opened the first bottle that evening." |
| Social media | One picture, one true sentence | "Lot AZ-0412, tasted this morning. Report due back from the laboratory next week." |
| Customer service | A person answering a person; say what you will do and by when | "Thank you for the photograph. A replacement leaves tomorrow; the tracking link will follow." |
| Packaging | Quiet. The lot number, the producer, a thank-you | "Chosen by the two of us. Lot AZ-0412. Thank you for having it at your table." |
| Order emails | Clear first, warm second | "Thank you, Anna. We have your order and will write again when it leaves." |

The examples with brackets are patterns, not copy: they must be filled from real records.

## 5. International SEO

### Where the site stands

**In place and correct**

- Ten locale addresses (en, en-gb, en-us, de-de, nl-nl, nl-be, fr-fr, fr-be, it-it, es-es) with `hreflang` and an `x-default`.
- A page that exists only in English is not announced as translated, and points search engines at its English address. This avoids duplicate, untranslated pages being indexed under other languages.
- Sitemap with language alternates; cart, checkout, account and admin kept out of search.
- Product structured data with price, currency and availability per market; breadcrumbs.
- One `h1` per page; descriptive link text; no third-party scripts slowing the page.

**Changed in this audit**

- Every page title now ends "— Azzopardi Olive Oil" (was "— Azzopardi"), so "olive oil" is in every title.
- A shared link now shows the logo (a 1200 × 630 picture) and the real page title, not just the word "Azzopardi".
- The FAQ carries question-and-answer structured data, which search engines can show directly in results.
- The home page tells search engines the organisation's name and logo.
- Search descriptions rewritten for the shop, contact, FAQ, journal and traceability pages.

**Open, in order of importance**

1. **Translate the pages that sell.** The home page, story, origins and FAQ exist only in English. Until they are translated by a native speaker, the shop cannot rank in German, Dutch, French, Italian or Spanish. Start with the home page and FAQ in the language of the first market you will ship to.
2. **Real products with specific names.** Search engines rank pages about something. "Extra Virgin Olive Oil" competes with the whole world; a named producer, region and variety does not.
3. **Photographs.** Products have no image, so they cannot appear in image or shopping results, and product structured data is incomplete without one.
4. **A registered domain and Search Console.** Nothing can be measured until the site is live and verified. Submit the sitemap on the first day.
5. **Reviews.** Once real reviews exist, they can be added to the product structured data. Never before.

### Search themes to build content around

These are themes, grouped by what the searcher wants. Volumes are not known. Each should be checked in a keyword tool for each language before writing.

| Intent | Themes (English) | Page that should answer it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy | high polyphenol olive oil; high polyphenol extra virgin olive oil; buy extra virgin olive oil online; early harvest olive oil | Home, shop, product pages |
| Buy by origin | Moroccan olive oil; Greek extra virgin olive oil; Turkish olive oil; Spanish / Italian single-variety olive oil | Origins page; a category or page per country once there are oils to list |
| Buy by variety | Picholine, Koroneiki, Picual, Coratina olive oil (only varieties you actually sell) | Product pages; a short page per variety |
| Understand | what are polyphenols in olive oil; what does extra virgin mean; why is olive oil bitter / peppery; how to read an olive oil lab report; acidity in olive oil | FAQ now; full journal guides next |
| Use | how to store olive oil; cooking with extra virgin olive oil; olive oil for bread, salad, fish | Journal recipes and guides |
| Trust | olive oil traceability; olive oil lot number; is my olive oil real | Quality & traceability page |
| Gift | olive oil gift; olive oil gift set | Only if a gift product exists |

In other languages the same themes apply, but the words must come from a native speaker and a keyword tool, not from translation: a German searcher types "Olivenöl mit hohem Polyphenolgehalt" or "polyphenolreiches Olivenöl", and which of the two matters.

### Content plan (journal)

Write these yourselves, in the first person, one at a time. Each answers a real question and links to the oils.

1. **How to read an olive oil laboratory report.** What each figure on your product pages means. Nobody else in the market explains this, and you publish the reports.
2. **What "extra virgin" means, and what it does not.**
3. **What polyphenols are, and why we publish the number.** Facts and the measured figure only. The wording must be reviewed by your food-law adviser before it goes live.
4. **Bitter and peppery: how to taste an olive oil at home.**
5. **How to store olive oil, and how long it keeps.**
6. **One article per producer:** a visit, in your words, with your photographs.
7. **One recipe a month:** something you really cook.

### Health claims and search

"High polyphenol olive oil" is searched largely by people looking for a health benefit. The site may say an oil is high in polyphenols and show the laboratory figure. It may not say what that does for health unless the claim is one the EU has authorised and the oil meets its conditions, and even then the exact wording is prescribed. Rules differ again in the United Kingdom and the United States. This is the single point where SEO ambition and the law pull in opposite directions; have the adviser approve the wording once, and reuse it.

### Links and reputation

Consistent with the brand, and in order of value: producers linking to their page on your site; food writers and cooks you send a bottle to with no conditions; local press about a couple starting a food business; honest listings in olive oil and specialty food directories. Do not buy links or reviews.

### What to measure, once live

Search Console: which queries show the site, in which country, and which pages they land on. Shop: how many visitors to a product page add to the cart, how many carts are paid, how many customers order twice. The last number matters most for a brand built on long relationships.

## 8. Correction: producers are not named (2026-11-13)

After this audit was written you told us that who produces your oils is secret. The audit above was written on the opposite assumption ("named producers", "the producer's name stays on it"), and those lines in it are out of date.

What was changed on the site:

- Every promise to name a producer is gone: home page, Our story, Where the oils come from, Harvest & production, Quality & traceability, Journal, the FAQ and the shop's description for search engines (six languages).
- The product page no longer has "Producer" or "Mill" rows. The lot lookup no longer shows "Grove" or "Mill".
- The shop's data and its API cannot carry a producer, a grove or a mill, even if one is recorded and was marked "name on the shop". A test fails if that changes.
- The customer assistant is not given the producer and is told to say that you do not share it.
- In the admin panel, producers remain as your own records, with a note that they are never shown.

What the site says in its place: that you follow the harvest season country by country, taste each lot, read its laboratory report, and show the country, region, olive varieties, dates and laboratory results for each oil.

What only you can check:

- **Laboratory reports.** A report published on a lot's page is the laboratory's own PDF. If it names the producer or the mill as the client, publishing it reveals them. Check each PDF before marking it verified, or ask the laboratory for a copy issued in your name.
- **Free-text fields.** "Origin", the product description and journal articles are typed by you. Nothing stops a name being typed there.
- **The label.** EU rules require the label to carry the name and address of the business responsible for the oil, and in some cases where it was bottled. That is a question for your food-law adviser, not something the website decides.
- **Item 12 of section 6** asked whether each producer agrees to be named. That no longer applies; the producer is still worth recording in the admin panel for your own traceability.
